# Biological identity and exposure adjudication

## What is established

The native checks use the deposited first model and highest-occupancy heavy-atom alternate, with file order resolving equal occupancy. Author chain names are explicitly converted through deposited label-chain/entity identities. All assembly generators here use identity operator 1; identities of biological assemblies matter even without a nonidentity transformation. In 6N4Y, A/F, B/E, C/H and D/G are four separate deposited receptor–Nb43 assemblies. An A/G contact in the asymmetric unit is not evidence for a second biological epitope. In 6N50, receptor author A and Nb43 author E (label D) form the VHH-containing assembly. No largest-contact or best-outcome chain selection is performed.

The mGlu source component remains intact. Nb43 sequences are identical in 6N4Y, 6N50, 6N51, 7DGE, 8T7H and 8TAO. The 7DGE supplement explicitly introduces seven mGlu5-like epitope substitutions in mGlu1 to recruit Nb43. This is engineered cross-receptor reuse, not an independent nanobody discovery. The 2019 full-length preparation refers to P41594-2; its canonical numbering must not be silently mixed with P41594 isoform 1. Deposited sequences and the reported termini/tags do not yet provide a reconciled complete cryo-EM/crystallization sample definition. [2019 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0881-4), [mGlu1 primary paper and supplement](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13238-020-00808-5).

The first 118 amino acids of the 7E9G DN13 and 7EPB nominal “Anti-RON” chains are identical; their exported tails differ. Original 7EPB paper evidence identifies DN13. The deposited Anti-RON label should not override that evidence or cause false exclusion. DN13 was discovered using immunized llamas and human/rat mGlu2 material. Both deposited complexes show DN13 contacting both receptor protomers. A one-receptor-chain geometry calculation would omit part of that epitope; no chain concatenation or selector change is made. Discovery lineage is not the complete 2021 reagent/tag sequence. [Discovery paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01489-1), [7EPB primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03641-w).

For M4, primary sample Methods and native geometry agree on a Gi/scFv16 assembly. Current GPCRdb M4 prefix 001_001_002 also matches retained development 4MQS/M2. Missing sequence-component flags in the old roster did not prove independence. C3aR entry 8IA8 and FFAR2 entry 8J23 likewise contain a two-domain scFv16 helper. A camelid annotation or antibody keyword is not sufficient to establish a VHH. The unresolved primary citations of these last two entries do not overturn their deposited no-VHH exclusions. [M4 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.83477), [M4 GPCRdb metadata](https://gpcrdb.org/services/protein/acm4_human/).

All seven LGR4 entries remain one source/receptor group, including the previously separate 9S38 component: its three polymer sequences exactly match 9S37. MB52 is a NB52-derived HopQ megabody directly binding LGR4, while NB18 and NB21 are distinct receptor-binding clones from the LGR4 source library. Library membership alone does not prove identical clonal ancestry, but the shared receptor already prevents independent counting. Partial megabody polymers, signal peptides, tags, and receptor discrepancies must be reconciled with the experimental sample before inputs are sealed. The 9S37/9S38 receptor differences K78A/W474A/T698G are annotated as conflicts, not established intentional mutations. The exact original-paper linkage for 9S38 remains unknown. [NB18 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-61545-z), [NB21 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-63410-5).

The recovered ADGRV1 primary preprint links 9FTE/EMD-50743, describes a mouse construct and direct RE02 binding. Its Methods boundary differs by one residue from the deposited sequence; the SGR linker also requires sample provenance. It reports AlphaFold-assisted reference model building. The original inaccessible-source record is preserved; successful text recovery is logged separately as rendered primary-mirror excerpts rather than original HTTP bytes. [Primary preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.05.709805).

The two mGlyR entries share Nb20, and the previously verified Nb20* mutant remains the same known lineage. Nb20 contacts both receptor protomers. The paper used an AlphaFold-generated Nb model and reports relatively weak/local density. For 9VOS, its 4.3 Å deposited resolution fails the existing gate even though the paper reports 3.89 Å. A globally acceptable number alone would still not certify interface quality. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68339-x).

## Exposure is not a single yes/no label

1. **Metadata screening:** all 26 were already inventoried. 7DGE, 7E9G, 7EPB, 8T7H and 8TAO had no individually logged structural-prose exposure in that inventory, but were already in the exposed mGlu source-family component. They were never certified unexposed.
2. **Earlier structural or prediction prose:** the 2019 mGlu entries had structural headings/model-building context recorded. LGR4 had native-contact and orientation prose. M4 had refinement/deposition context; 8IA8 had a contact snippet; 8J23 had structural-caption context. mGlyR had structural navigation/prose and sequence/lineage work. For 9FTE, the September 7 addendum is stronger than the September 4 record: actual contact assignments and prediction prose were seen. The earlier, narrower exposure description must not be substituted for that later event.
3. **Current structural inspection:** all 26 native deposits, native interchain contacts and residue maps were opened in this authorized review after the implementation freeze. Accessible primary Methods, supplements and relevant biological Results were reviewed. Source retrieval also rendered surrounding biological structural prose. This is eligibility inspection; it is now recorded as native exposure and will remain visible in any future evaluation.
4. **ConfoVHH method-development use:** none of these 26 is marked a direct development case in the retained inventory, and this review made no scoring changes. That is narrower than proving the full absence of development influence. The established M4–M2 source-family link independently disqualifies those four entries. For the remaining families, the protocol explicitly keeps exposure families blocked. This review does not waive that rule or silently rename structural prose as mere metadata.
5. **Predictor-training exposure:** target-specific membership in the pinned Boltz training/distillation/template inputs is not established or excluded here. Publication date alone cannot certify novelty. AlphaFold assistance in experimental reference modeling is separately documented for 9FTE and mGlyR; it is not evidence that a particular Boltz checkpoint was trained on those complexes.

`reused-evidence.json` binds the original exposure records, and each row of `decisions.json` preserves its prior status/source paths separately from this review. No group can be cleared by a newly supplied plasmid alone while its exposure gate remains unresolved. If those families are used in future, an explicitly versioned exposure-aware evaluation would be required; it must not be presented as independent validation under the unchanged protocol.
